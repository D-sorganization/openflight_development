# Camera / Photometric Golf Launch Monitor Technology — Research Dossier

Scope: Foresight GC2/GC3/GCQuad/GCHawk/QuadMAX/Falcon, Bushnell Launch Pro, SkyTrak/SkyTrak+/ST MAX, Uneekor QED/EYE XO/XO2/XR/EYE MINI, ProTee VX, Full Swing KIT, Trackman iO (camera aspects), Rapsodo MLM2PRO, Garmin R50, and DIY/open-source systems (PiTrac, OpenFlight, etc.).

---

## 1. Imaging Architecture

### 1.1 Foresight Sports family (the reference photometric design)

**Camera counts and generations.** The GC2 ("Game Changer 2", launched at the 2010 PGA Show) was named for its **stereoscopic two-camera** system; club data required the add-on **HMT (Head Measurement Technology)** module, making a GC2+HMT effectively a quadrascopic system — "two on the GC2 capturing data about ball flight and two on the HMT capturing data about clubhead movement and impact" ([Foresight GC2+HMT info page](https://foresightsportseu.zendesk.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360017513057-GC2-HMT-Information-Page), [Golfstead GC2 review](https://golfstead.com/foresight-sports-gc2-launch-monitor-review)). The **GC3 / Bushnell Launch Pro** use a **"Triscopic" three-camera** system (identical hardware between the two brands, the Launch Pro being a Foresight-built rebadge) ([PlayBetter comparison](https://www.playbetter.com/blogs/golf/bushnell-launch-pro-vs-gc3-vs-gc-quad-comparison), [HoleThePutt](https://holetheputt.com/bushnell-launch-pro-vs-gc3-launch-monitor-comparison/)). The **GCQuad** (2017) and **QuadMAX** (2024) use a **"Quadrascopic" four-camera** array with cameras at the four corners of the sensor window; the ceiling-mounted **GCHawk** uses the same quadrascopic optical package pointed downward over a 52″×30″ hitting zone (9.5 ft ceiling minimum, 10–10.5 ft recommended) ([Foresight GCQuad](https://www.foresightsports.com/products/gcquad-launch-monitor), [Foresight Sports EU GCHawk](https://foresightsports.eu/product/gchawk-launch-monitor/), [Home Performance Lab GCHawk review](https://homeperformancelab.com/foresight-gchawk-review/), [Carl's Place Foresight comparison](https://www.carlofet.com/blog/foresight-launch-monitor-comparison)).

**Frame rates and capture volume.** Marketing and reseller material consistently cite very high burst frame rates: the GCQuad's lenses "simultaneously capture up to 10,000 frames per second," taking roughly **200 images of ball and club per shot** within the **first ~30 cm of ball flight** ([Golfstead GCQuad review](https://golfstead.com/foresight-sports-gcquad-launch-monitor-review), [Foresight Sports EU GCQuad](https://foresightsports.eu/launch-monitors/gcquad/), [Golfers Authority](https://golfersauthority.com/foresight-gcquad-launch-monitor/)); other sources cite ~6,000 fps for the four-camera burst ([Ace Indoor Golf](https://aceindoorgolf.com/products/foresight-sports-gcquad-launch-monitor)). The key architectural point: these are **short-burst, IR-strobed, high-speed exposures over a small capture volume next to the tee**, not continuous video — the ball is fully characterized in the first foot of flight.

**Illumination and triggering.** Foresight units combine **infrared object tracking** (detecting the presence of a teed ball and inbound clubhead in the hitting zone) with the high-speed camera burst — the IR subsystem arms the cameras and triggers the burst as the club enters the capture zone ([Foresight EU GCQuad](https://foresightsports.eu/launch-monitors/gcquad/), [Golfstead](https://golfstead.com/foresight-sports-gcquad-launch-monitor-review)). Units are self-contained: onboard IR illumination means no external lighting or high-speed studio strobes are needed, and Foresight claims "no calibration needed" out of the box for the camera rig (factory-calibrated intrinsics/extrinsics) ([Rain or Shine](https://rainorshinegolf.com/products/gcquad-launch-monitor-by-foresight-sports-new)).

### 1.2 Uneekor overhead systems

The **EYE XO / EYE XO2** are ceiling-mounted (~9–10 ft high, ~3.5 ft ahead of the tee) and look **straight down** at the hitting area with **two high-speed IR cameras operating at 3,000+ fps**; the XO2 adds a **third infrared camera** ([Carl's Place EYE XO](https://shop.carlofet.com/uneekor-eye-xo), [Golfstead EYE XO review](https://golfstead.com/uneekor-eye-xo-launch-monitor-review), [Top Shelf Golf XO2 review](https://topshelfgolf.com/blogs/golf/uneekor-eye-xo2-review)). The older **QED** is also overhead dual-camera but requires **marked balls** for spin. The **EYE MINI** is a portable floor unit beside the ball with **two high-speed infrared cameras recording over 3,000 fps** plus an E-ink status display ([Uneekor EYE MINI](https://uneekor.com/golf-launch-monitors/eye-mini), [ShopIndoorGolf](https://shopindoorgolf.com/products/uneekor-eye-mini-launch-monitor)). The **EYE XR** is a rear/floor-mounted dual-camera + IR-sensor system using the same Club & Dimple Optix stack ([Uneekor EYE XR](https://uneekor.com/golf-launch-monitors/eye-xr)).

### 1.3 ProTee VX

Ceiling-mounted overhead unit from ProTee United (Dutch, ~20 years in simulators): **two synchronized high-speed cameras + built-in IR illumination**, with a machine-learning/AI shot-analysis pipeline delivering **25 data points in <0.3 s**; no club stickers, no marked balls, no minimum distance to screen ([PlayBetter ProTee VX review](https://www.playbetter.com/blogs/golf-simulator-reviews/protee-vx-review), [Carl's Place VX](https://shop.carlofet.com/protee-vx-launch-monitor), [Top Shelf Golf VX review](https://topshelfgolf.com/blogs/golf/protee-vx-review-2026)).

### 1.4 Hybrid radar+camera systems

- **Trackman iO** (ceiling-mounted, indoor-only): "Optically Enhanced Radar Tracking" (OERT) — Doppler radar + embedded IR lighting + **dual high-speed cameras at 4,600 fps** that **directly measure 3D spin** optically; the optical layer "locks radar data to high-speed image capture," sharpening club/impact measurement without ball stickers or club tape ([Rain or Shine iO](https://rainorshinegolf.com/products/trackman-io-launch-monitor), [Trackman launch monitors](https://www.trackman.com/golf/launch-monitors), [SwingSense iO article](https://swingsense.com/trackman-io-launch-monitor-the-technology-elevating-your-golf/)). Trackman 4's OERT similarly fuses a built-in camera with dual radar.
- **SkyTrak+ / ST MAX**: original SkyTrak was purely photometric (ball-only); the **SkyTrak+** added **dual Doppler radar for club data** (club speed, path, face angle, smash) while an improved photometric camera system measures ball launch and spin from images at/just after impact, with "proprietary machine-learning algorithms" fusing the two ([SkyTrak ST+ measurement page](https://www.skytrakgolf.com/pages/what-does-the-skytrak-st-launch-monitor-measure), [SkyTrak+ product page](https://www.skytrakgolf.com/products/skytrak-plus-launch-monitor), [ST MAX Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/SKYTRAK-MAX-Launch-Monitor-Photometric/dp/B0FXNBJFG3)).
- **Full Swing KIT**: primarily a **patented 24 GHz dual-mode, ML-enhanced radar** ("5D machine-learning radar"); the built-in 4K camera is used for swing video and ML vision that "combines radar data with image processing," claiming accuracy within 1–2% of TrackMan — i.e., the camera assists rather than performing photogrammetric measurement ([Full Swing KIT technology page](https://www.fullswinggolf.com/kit-launch-monitor-technology/), [ShopIndoorGolf KIT](https://shopindoorgolf.com/products/full-swing-kit-launch-monitor)).
- **Rapsodo MLM2PRO**: **Doppler radar + two 240 fps cameras** ("Impact Vision"); cameras photograph the ball at and just after impact ([Golfstead MLM2PRO review](https://golfstead.com/rapsodo-mlm2pro-launch-monitor-review), [Top Shelf Golf MLM2PRO review](https://topshelfgolf.com/blogs/golf/rapsodo-mlm2pro-review-2026)).
- **Garmin Approach R50**: **three high-speed cameras arranged horizontally** (vs. the GC3's vertical stack) in a side-of-player floor unit; purely optical measurement of 15+ ball and club metrics, plus a 10″ touchscreen running the sim ([Garmin R50 product page](https://www.garmin.com/en-US/p/736810/), [Golf.com R50 first look](https://golf.com/gear/garmin-approach-r50-launch-monitor/), [Golfstead R50 review](https://golfstead.com/garmin-approach-r50-launch-monitor-review)).

## 2. Ball Measurement (speed, launch, spin)

**Photogrammetric core.** Multi-camera photometric units capture a synchronized burst of stereo images of the ball over the first ~1 ft of flight. Because camera intrinsics/extrinsics are factory-known, each image pair/quad yields a 3D ball-center position; **ball speed** is the displacement between timestamped 3D positions, **launch angle** is the vertical component of the initial velocity vector, and **side/launch direction** the horizontal component relative to the calibrated target line ([Foresight EU ball & club data](https://foresightsports.eu/ball-club-data/), [PlayBetter photometric vs radar explainer](https://www.playbetter.com/blogs/golf-simulator-comparisons/photometric-vs-radar-golf-launch-monitor)).

**Spin via dimple-pattern tracking ("markerless").** The flagship photometric approach is to resolve the **dimple pattern** on the ball in successive frames and solve for the 3D rotation that maps one frame's pattern to the next. Foresight calls this **Spherical Correlation™**: "the onboard computer will then compare each image to the next, and using a method called spherical correlation, it will measure how the dimple pattern is rotating throughout the image set," yielding total spin **and** spin-tilt axis directly ([Golf Swing Systems GCQuad page](https://www.golfswingsystems.co.uk/product/foresight-sports-gcquad/), [Foresight EU GCQuad](https://foresightsports.eu/launch-monitors/gcquad/)). Uneekor's patented equivalent is **Dimple Optix™**, which "reads the actual dimple pattern on your golf ball to calculate spin" with any unmarked ball ([Uneekor blog: Photometric vs Doppler](https://uneekor.com/blogs/blog/photometric-vs.-doppler:-which-launch-monitor-technology-delivers-the-most-accurate-golf-data), [Carl's Place EYE XO](https://shop.carlofet.com/uneekor-eye-xo)).

**Marked-ball spin (legacy / budget path).** Where camera resolution or geometry can't resolve dimples reliably, systems track high-contrast printed features instead: Uneekor **QED requires marked balls** to compute spin ([Golfstead QED review](https://golfstead.com/uneekor-qed-launch-monitor-review)); Rapsodo's **RPT (Rapsodo Precision Technology) balls** carry printed markers that the MLM2PRO's cameras track to report spin rate and axis "within 1% of high-end launch monitors" — with ordinary balls it falls back to estimated/algorithmic spin ([Top Shelf Golf MLM2PRO review](https://topshelfgolf.com/blogs/golf/rapsodo-mlm2pro-review-2026), [Golf Simulator Advisor](https://golfsimulatoradvisor.com/rapsodo-mlm2pro-review/)). This marked-vs-markerless split is the single clearest technology tier divider in the market.

**Radar contrast.** Doppler-only units estimate spin from decay/trajectory signatures or read it only with ball flight room; camera systems measure it in the first foot. Indoors "the less ball flight you can provide, the less information the radar can capture before the ball hits the screen," pushing radar toward estimation on spin and shot shape ([GolfBays radar vs photometric](https://golfbays.de/en/blogs/news/radar-vs-photometric-launch-monitors-indoors), [PlayBetter explainer](https://www.playbetter.com/blogs/golf-simulator-comparisons/photometric-vs-radar-golf-launch-monitor)).

## 3. Club Measurement

### 3.1 Foresight fiducial-marker approach (direct measurement)

Foresight systems (GCQuad/QuadMAX/GCHawk with club-data license; historically GC2+HMT; Bushnell Launch Pro with paid club add-on) require **reflective fiducial stickers on the clubface**. The HMT documentation is explicit about the dot-count → data mapping: **1 dot** → club speed, efficiency, path, angle of attack; **4 dots** → full data including dynamic loft, lie, face angle, closure rate, and **impact location on the face** ([Foresight HMT support](https://www.foresightsports.com/hmt-head-measurement), [HMT marker application guide](https://help.foresightsports.com/hc/en-us/articles/4408197030035-How-to-Apply-and-Maintain-Club-Markers-for-Foresight-Sports-Devices), [marker troubleshooting](https://help.foresightsports.com/hc/en-us/articles/4405916455443-Troubleshooting-Club-Data-and-Marker-Detection-on-Foresight-Sports-Launch-Monitors)). Mechanism: the IR strobe makes the retroreflective dots bloom brightly in each high-speed frame; triangulating the dot constellation across cameras and frames gives the **6-DOF pose of the clubface through impact**, from which club speed (pose translation rate), path and attack angle (velocity direction), face angle/dynamic loft/lie (face-plane orientation), and **closure rate** ("how fast the club face is closing in three dimensions, in degrees per second") are computed. **Impact location** is the measured ball position at contact expressed in the face's coordinate frame — "the precise measurement of the contact point of the golf ball on the club face, relative to the face centre, is determined by the fiducial placement" ([Foresight EU ball & club data](https://foresightsports.eu/ball-club-data/)). Foresight argues this front-positioned direct face imaging is inherently better than radar "positioned behind the golfer [with] no direct view of the club face" ([same source](https://foresightsports.eu/ball-club-data/); [Foresight vs Trackman blog](https://www.foresightsports.com/blogs/golf-tips/foresight-sports-vs-trackman-learning-the-key-differences)).

GC3/Launch Pro (3 cameras) delivers club speed, path, face angle, attack angle and impact via stickers but **omits dynamic loft, lie, and closure rate**, which remain GCQuad/QuadMAX exclusives ([PlayBetter comparison](https://www.playbetter.com/blogs/golf/bushnell-launch-pro-vs-gc3-vs-gc-quad-comparison), [Indoor Golf Outlet GC3 vs GCQuad](https://indoorgolfoutlet.com/blogs/main/gc3-vs-gcquad)). Garmin's R50 likewise "needs reflective markers (known as fiducials in the fitting world) placed on the clubhead to receive clubhead data" ([Golf.com R50](https://golf.com/gear/garmin-approach-r50-launch-monitor/)).

### 3.2 Uneekor sticker-free overhead club tracking

Because EYE XO/XO2 cameras look **down** at the clubhead, they can segment the clubhead silhouette optically without stickers: club data "is captured optically without reflective dots or tape," and **Club Optix™ / Swing Optix** deliver actual high-speed video replay of the clubhead striking the ball, including face-on impact visuals and impact-location feedback ([Rain or Shine EYE XO](https://rainorshinegolf.com/products/uneekor-eyexo-golf-simulator), [Golfstead EYE XO review](https://golfstead.com/uneekor-eye-xo-launch-monitor-review)). Note the portable **EYE MINI does require club stickers** for club data — the side-view geometry loses the overhead advantage ([PlayBetter EYE MINI review](https://www.playbetter.com/blogs/golf-simulator-reviews/uneekor-eye-mini-review)). ProTee VX also claims full sticker-free club data (speed, path, face angle, face-to-path, attack angle, dynamic loft, lie, vertical & horizontal impact point) from its overhead AI vision ([GolfSimDepot VX](https://golfsimdepot.com/products/protee-united-vx-launch-monitor), [Indoor Golf Pros VX](https://www.indoorgolfpros.com/products/protee-vx-launch-monitor)).

## 4. Calibration and Alignment

- **Factory intrinsics/extrinsics:** Foresight ships units "with no calibration needed" — the multi-camera rig is rigidly fixed and factory calibrated; users only align the unit to the target line (lens face parallel/perpendicular conventions per model) ([Rain or Shine GCQuad](https://rainorshinegolf.com/products/gcquad-launch-monitor-by-foresight-sports-new), [Foresight hardware support](https://help.foresightsports.com/hc/en-us/categories/360001929313-Hardware)).
- **Uneekor overhead calibration** is the most involved in the class: a printed **calibration chart/board** is placed on the mat, leveled (front-back and side-side with a bubble level), squared to the impact screen, and its crosshair/circle aligned to red crosshairs overlaid on the live CENTER CAM / SIDE CAM feeds in the software; this establishes the ground plane, tee zone, and target-line extrinsics for the ceiling-mounted cameras ([Carl's Place Uneekor calibration guide](https://www.carlofet.com/blog/calibrating-uneekor-launch-monitors), [Uneekor EYE XO2 calibration guide PDF](https://download.uneekor.com/docs/EYEXO2_Calibration_Guide.pdf), [Golf Simulator Videos EYE XO calibration](https://golfsimulatorvideos.com/2020/11/how-to-calibrate-uneekor-eye-xo-plus-troubleshooting-setup-tips/)).
- **DIY practice mirrors commercial practice:** PiTrac ships Python/shell utilities for **camera and lens (distortion) calibration** and is building **auto-calibration** tooling ([PiTrac docs](https://docs.pitrac.org/), [Hackaday.io details](https://hackaday.io/project/195042-pitrac-the-diy-golf-launch-monitor/details)).

## 5. Accuracy: Claims, Independent Data, and the Indoor Argument

- **Peer-reviewed validation:** Leach et al. (2017) remains the key published criterion study — Trackman Pro IIIe and **Foresight GC2+HMT** vs. a high-speed video reference; both were deemed valid for field use, with median differences on the order of −0.4 mph clubhead speed, 0.2 mph ball speed, 0.0° launch angle ([summary via Trackman 4 reliability paper, Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2024.2314864)). The 2024 Trackman 4 paper itself documents within/between-session reliability of radar metrics indoors for high-level golfers ([tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2024.2314864)).
- **Spin repeatability:** In Foresight's own head-to-head protocol (echoed by third parties), GCQuad center-strike **spin-rate standard deviation ≈ 82 RPM vs. ~175 RPM for TrackMan 4** — camera systems measure spin more consistently than radar; GC3/BLP path and face angle benchmark within 1–2% of GCQuad in independent testing ([Foresight GCQuad vs GC3 blog](https://www.foresightsports.com/blogs/golf-tips/comparing-golf-launch-monitor-foresight-sports-gcquad-and-gc3-stand-above-the-rest), [Golfers Authority QuadMAX/GCQuad/TrackMan comparison](https://golfersauthority.com/quadmax-vs-gcquad-vs-trackman/), [PlayBetter BLP comparison](https://www.playbetter.com/blogs/golf/bushnell-launch-pro-vs-gc3-vs-gc-quad-comparison)).
- **The structural indoor advantage:** photometric units **measure actual launch conditions** (speed, angles, 3D spin) in the first foot of flight and then **model** the full trajectory with a ball-flight model; nothing is lost when the ball hits a screen 8 ft away. Radar indoors must infer from a truncated flight, degrading spin/shape estimates; typical figures quoted are ±1% (photometric) vs ±1–2% (radar) ball speed indoors ([Uneekor photometric-vs-Doppler blog](https://uneekor.com/blogs/blog/photometric-vs.-doppler:-which-launch-monitor-technology-delivers-the-most-accurate-golf-data), [solheimcup2019 tech comparison](https://solheimcup2019.com/radar-vs-photometric-launch-monitors/), [GolfBays](https://golfbays.de/en/blogs/news/radar-vs-photometric-launch-monitors-indoors), [Austad's](https://austads.com/blogs/blog/camera-vs-radar-launch-monitors-which-is-better)). Conversely, radar's strength is outdoors, tracking the entire real flight.
- Corporate/engineering background on Foresight's photometric design philosophy: [Popular Science — "From CAD to caddies"](https://www.popsci.com/gear/foresight-sports-quadmax-photometric-golf-launch-monitor-development/).

- **Branded/patented methods:** Foresight **Spherical Correlation™** (dimple-rotation solving), Uneekor **Dimple Optix™** (patented markerless dimple spin) and **Club/Swing Optix**, Full Swing's **patented 24 GHz dual-mode ML radar**, Trackman **OERT** (sources above).
- Representative patent-literature touchpoints found in this research: [US 11,565,166 — golf ball tracking and scoring](https://patents.justia.com/patent/11565166); [US 12,145,675 — mobile golf simulation system (references FSX)](https://patents.justia.com/patent/12145675); [USPTO 12,401,909 — timing determination in camera-based motion measurement, which explicitly discusses applying **Gabor filters** (via OpenCV) to golf-ball dimples for spin tracking](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12401909). ## 7. DIY / Open-Source Projects (most relevant to OpenFlight)

**PiTrac** — the flagship open-source photometric build ([GitHub PiTracLM/PiTrac, GPL-2.0](https://github.com/PiTracLM/PiTrac), [docs.pitrac.org](https://docs.pitrac.org/), [Hackaday.io project](https://hackaday.io/project/195042-pitrac-the-diy-golf-launch-monitor)):
- **Architecture:** Raspberry Pi(s) + **Pi Global Shutter cameras (~$50 each)**; instead of expensive multi-kfps cameras it uses **IR strobe-based multi-exposure capture** — an IR LED array pulses several times within a single long exposure, freezing multiple ball positions in one frame. One camera watches the teed ball (trigger/launch detection); the second captures the strobed in-flight images. A **custom PCB** (a few dollars) drives strobe/trigger; total BOM ≈ **$250–300**, with a Pi 5 consolidation to a single board underway ([Hackaday details](https://hackaday.io/project/195042-pitrac-the-diy-golf-launch-monitor/details), [PiTrac home](https://pitraclm.github.io/PiTrac/)).
- **Pipeline:** C++ core with OpenCV; **HoughCircles()** locates the teed ball, the strobed in-flight ball images, and centers/radii for spin analysis; velocity/angles from strobe-timestamped 3D positions; **3D spin (all 3 axes, to ~1° angular displacement resolution)** solved by searching candidate rotations that best register dimple imagery between strobe exposures a few ms apart (Gabor-filter dimple enhancement is the documented technique in the adjacent patent literature) ([Hackaday project logs](https://hackaday.io/project/195042/logs?sort=oldest&page=2), [USPTO 12,401,909](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12401909)).
- **Calibration & integration:** camera/lens distortion-correction and auto-calibration utilities; outputs to a web dashboard and **GSPro and E6/TruGolf** interfaces ([docs.pitrac.org](https://docs.pitrac.org/)).
- **Status:** self-described "really immature… basic features usually work reliably"; active Discord community ([GitHub README](https://github.com/PiTracLM/PiTrac)).

**OpenFlight** ([jewbetcha/openflight](https://github.com/jewbetcha/openflight)) — open-source launch monitor using **Doppler radar** (ball speed, club speed, launch angle, spin, carry), documented in [Grafana Labs' write-up](https://medium.com/grafana-labs/openflight-building-an-open-source-golf-launch-monitor-with-raspberry-pi-grafana-cloud-and-84d68ad40fc5) and a [GrafanaCON recap](https://tldrecap.tech/posts/2026/grafanacon/openflight-open-source-golf-launch-monitor/); notable finding that radar can detect spin via the spinning ball's Doppler "wobble."

**ronheywood/opencv** ([GitHub](https://github.com/ronheywood/opencv)) — Python/OpenCV experiments explicitly imitating the **GC2's flash-duration multi-exposure technique** to derive launch angle, ball speed, spin and spin axis with commodity hardware.

**Key transferable lessons for OpenFlight:** (1) strobed multi-exposure with a cheap global-shutter sensor substitutes for kfps cameras; (2) markerless spin needs dimple-resolving resolution in the capture volume — otherwise use marked balls (Rapsodo RPT model); (3) impact/club data without stickers requires overhead geometry (Uneekor/ProTee) or fiducials (Foresight/Garmin); (4) factory-rigid stereo + a floor calibration target (Uneekor-style chart) is the practical calibration pattern; (5) photometric measurement + ball-flight model is the correct indoor architecture — measure launch, simulate flight.
