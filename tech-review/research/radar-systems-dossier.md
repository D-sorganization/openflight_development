# Doppler Radar Golf Launch Monitor Technology — Research Dossier

## 1. Radar Physics Fundamentals

### 1.1 CW Doppler principle

All the units in scope are built around continuous-wave (CW) Doppler radar: a microwave carrier is transmitted, reflections from the moving ball/club are frequency-shifted by the Doppler effect, and the shift is proportional to the radial velocity of the reflecting point: f_dopp = (2/λ)·V_rad. For a spinning ball, each surface point has its own radial velocity, so the return is a velocity *spectrum*, not a single line — the foundation of radar spin measurement (TrackMan/Tuxen patent US 8,845,442, https://patents.google.com/patent/US8845442B2/en; overview at https://www.golfspan.com/how-do-golf-launch-monitors-work).

### 1.2 Getting 3D position from a Doppler radar: multi-receiver phase interferometry

A single CW radar gives radial velocity only. All serious units use **multiple receive antennas** and measure the **phase difference** of the return between receiver pairs (phase-comparison monopulse / interferometry) to obtain the two angular coordinates of the target; combined with integrated radial velocity (and/or FMCW range), this yields a full 3D track. Tuxen's spin-axis patent US 10,850,179 explicitly describes "phase-phase monopulse comparison... or interferometry" across receivers 106A/B/C, with angular positions obtained from phase differences of receiver pairs (https://patents.google.com/patent/US10850179B2/en). The Garmin R10 uses a three-receiver radar in the same way (https://mygolfsimulator.com/garmin-r10-data/). FlightScope describes its aperture as a **phased antenna array** that both steers/receives over a wide arc and resolves the return electronically (https://flightscope.com/products/mevo-plus; https://www.cerogolf.com/post/what-is-flightscope-and-what-does-it-measure). FlightScope additionally advertises "multi-frequency radar with **direct distance measurement**" on the X3 — i.e., not pure CW (https://thegolfwire.com/326713-2/).

### 1.3 CW + FMCW hybrid architectures

Full Swing's launch-monitor patent (US 2020/0147470, https://www.freepatentsonline.com/y2020/0147470.html) is the clearest public description of a hybrid: two transmitters at ~24 GHz **time-division multiplexed, one CW and one linear-FMCW**. CW signals provide club speed, ball speed, spin rate/axis and launch-window angles from frequency shifts; the FMCW chirp provides direct range ("accurate out to 250 m or more"), carry and side displacement from phase/beat-frequency measurements. The patented antenna layout is a **non-uniform array — four receive antennas symmetric about the axis, two transmit antennas placed asymmetrically** — claimed to produce "a richer receive signal of phase and frequency shifts," with spin extracted from the number, spacing and amplitude of FFT peaks. This is the basis of the Full Swing KIT's "dual-mode 24 GHz radar" marketing (https://www.fullswinggolf.com/kit-specs/).

### 1.5 TrackMan's dual-radar architecture

TrackMan 4 runs two synchronized (in time and space) radar systems: an "ultra-high-frequency"/high-resolution short-range radar dedicated to **club and impact** (path, face angle, attack angle, spin loft) and a high-accuracy long-range radar for **ball flight from launch to landing** (spin rate, curvature) (https://golfrange.org/trackman-introduces-dual-radar-technology/; https://www.trackman.com/golf/launch-monitors/tech-specs). Receivers sample at 40,000 samples/s to pin down the moment of impact (https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/two-radars-one-camera-zero-doubt). Company history: founded 2003 by the Eldrup-Jørgensen brothers with CTO Fredrik Tuxen, previously a missile/projectile Doppler-tracking engineer at Weibel Scientific (https://www.golfmonthly.com/features/from-tracking-missiles-to-tracking-golf-balls-meet-the-man-that-you-didnt-realise-changed-golf; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TrackMan).

## 2. Ball Data Measurement

### 2.1 Ball speed, launch angles

Ball speed comes directly from the Doppler shift of the dominant spectral line; vertical and horizontal launch angles come from the interferometric 3D track immediately after impact (§1.2). These are the *most directly measured* quantities and show the best inter-device agreement in every independent study (Leach et al., see §6).

### 2.2 Spin rate — harmonic sideband method (Tuxen patent US 8,845,442 / EP 1 698 380)

The key insight: a golf ball is not a perfect sphere (dimples, seams, print, internal asymmetry), so as it spins the reflection is periodically modulated. The Doppler spectrum shows the main velocity line flanked by **equally spaced sidebands with multiple harmonics, spaced exactly at the spin frequency ω/2π**. A surface point's Doppler contribution is F_dopp,B = (2/λ)·(V_rad − r·ω·sin ωt), i.e., frequency modulation with deviation (2/λ)·r·ω. Signal-processing chain per the patent: (1) track the central velocity line; (2) detect symmetric sideband peaks; (3) track harmonic traces over time; (4) *qualify* them by verifying equidistant spacing across consecutive FFT frames; (5) solve the harmonic-number assignment; (6) spin rate = trace frequency ÷ harmonic number (https://patents.google.com/patent/US8845442B2/en; TrackMan's own explainer: https://blog.trackmangolf.com/patent-measuring-the-spin-of-a-sports-ball/). This patent (EP 1 698 380) was litigated successfully against FlightScope's distributor in Düsseldorf in 2013 and upheld by the German Federal Court of Justice (https://thegolfwire.com/322771-2/, https://thegolfwire.com/329120-2/). Practical corollary: sideband strength depends on ball surface features — hence degraded radar spin fidelity on pristine range balls and the emergence of metal-marked "RCT" balls (§5.4).

### 2.3 Spin axis

Two families of methods:

1. **Trajectory inversion (US 8,845,442):** with total acceleration A from the differentiated 3D track, A = G + D + L; drag is the component of (A−G) along the airspeed vector, D = [(A−G)·V_air/|V_air|²]·V_air; lift L = A − G − D; and since Magnus lift is perpendicular to the spin axis (L·ω_e = 0), stacking this orthogonality constraint over many trajectory points gives an overdetermined linear system for the spin-axis unit vector, assuming the axis is quasi-constant relative to the lift-direction variation (patent link above). This is why spin axis is fundamentally a *derived* quantity for radar units, and why it degrades indoors where only 8–20 ft of flight is observed.
2. **Direct spectral/interferometric methods (US 10,850,179 and continuations US 11,446,546, US 11,938,375):** using per-receiver-pair phase differences to locate the reflection points and the principal axis of the spinning ball (https://patents.google.com/patent/US10850179B2/en).

Note "spin axis" as reported is the D-plane tilt driving curvature; Tuxen's rule of thumb: to finish on the target line the spin-axis tilt must be ≈2.5× the horizontal launch angle with opposite sign (https://www.thediygolfer.com/blog/trackman-golf-for-dummies).

### 2.4 Tracked flight vs. modeled carry

Outdoors, TrackMan tracks the entire ~6 s flight and reports *measured* carry/apex/landing angle (https://shop.carlofet.com/trackman-4-launch-monitor). Indoors, every radar unit observes only the pre-screen segment (TrackMan 4 needs ≥4.7 m unit-to-net; https://www.trackman.com/golf/launch-monitors/tech-specs) and extrapolates with an aerodynamic model fitted to its measured-flight database; spin axis in particular "has to be inferred when the ball hits the screen before full flight can be tracked" (https://homeperformancelab.com/trackman-simulator-data/). FlightScope claims Fusion Tracking needs just 8 ft of ball travel indoors (https://rainorshinegolf.com/products/flightscope-x3c-swingbay-golf-simulator-package). The R10 needs ≥20 m of visible flight and ball speed >90 mph to *measure* spin at all; below that (and generally indoors without an RCT ball) spin is estimated by a machine-learned model from measured radar inputs; outdoors it derives spin from observed trajectory curvature (https://mygolfsimulator.com/garmin-r10-accuracy/; https://topshelfgolf.com/blogs/golf/skytrak-st-max-vs-garmin-approach-r10).

## 3. Club Data: Measured vs. Inferred

### 3.1 What the radar actually tracks on the clubhead

A clubhead is a large, complex reflector whose parts move at different speeds — heel-to-toe speed differences reach ~7 mph. TrackMan defines Club Speed as the linear speed of the **geometric center** of the clubhead just before first contact, and states it "directly measures the 3D silhouette of the club head" to locate that center (typically <6 mm from the CG) rather than reporting the fastest-moving reflection (https://support.trackmangolf.com/hc/en-us/articles/39724348308891-Parameters-Club-Speed-Tee-to-Green; https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/what-is-club-speed). This is a real differentiator: naive Doppler units tend to lock to the strongest/fastest return (often the toe for a driver), inflating club speed — one reason club-speed disagreement between brands is chronic (https://forums.golfwrx.com/topic/1431294-Trackman-and-Flightscope-speed-numbers).

### 3.2 TrackMan's club-delivery parameter set and reference points

Per TrackMan's definitions: **swing direction, swing plane, low point** are referenced to the geometric center's trajectory; **face angle and dynamic loft** are measured as the orientation of the face *at the center-point of ball contact* (impact location) at maximum compression; **spin loft and face-to-path** are computed at both reference points; **attack angle and club path** are the vertical/horizontal direction of geometric-center motion at impact (https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/club-data-definitions; https://support.trackmangolf.com/hc/en-us/articles/5089892383515-Practice-Trackman-Data-Parameter-Definitions). Spin Loft is *calculated* (3D angle between club-motion direction and face normal; ≈ dynamic loft − attack angle only when face-to-path ≈ 0) (https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/spin-loft).

### 3.3 Face angle: direct or inverted from ball data?

Historically (pre-TM4/OERT), pure-radar face angle and dynamic loft were effectively obtained by **D-plane inversion**: TrackMan's own 2009 ball-flight-laws newsletter quantified horizontal launch direction as ≈85% face angle / 15% club path for a driver, and the face orientation was reconstructed from measured launch direction, launch angle and spin within the D-plane model (https://www.yumpu.com/en/document/view/11652756/trackman-ball-flight-laws; https://www.thediygolfer.com/blog/trackman-golf-for-dummies). On TrackMan 4 with OERT, the company's position is that club delivery (including face angle at the contact point) is measured via the synchronized radar+camera silhouette tracking rather than back-computed from ball flight (https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/two-radars-one-camera-zero-doubt). Third parties echo that face angle/club path are "genuine measurements... not estimates derived from where the ball went" for TM4/iO (https://homeperformancelab.com/trackman-simulator-data/) — though the exact measurement/estimation split remains proprietary. On consumer units the hierarchy is clearly weaker: Garmin specs face angle at ±2° and classifies it as algorithm-calculated, vs ±1° for measured launch direction (https://mygolfsimulator.com/garmin-r10-accuracy/).

### 3.4 Known club-data limitations

- Independent benchmarking (Leach et al. 2017, §6) found **ball parameters agree far better than clubhead parameters** for both TrackMan (radar) and Foresight GC2+HMT; researchers are cautioned on club data (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0263224117305079).
- Irons vs driver: shaft/hosel returns, turf interaction and lower speeds make club-path/face-angle pickup noisier for short clubs; TrackMan's OERT ">90% club-data pickup rate" claim is itself an acknowledgment pickup is not universal (https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/two-radars-one-camera-zero-doubt).
- Radar-only consumer devices: R10 club path is measured but noisy and geometry-sensitive (alignment errors bias face/path systematically) (https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/golf/f/approach-r10/305298/outdoor-range-and-club-path-questions).

## 4. TrackMan OERT (Optically Enhanced Radar Tracking)

TrackMan 4 (and iO) fuse the dual radar with a built-in camera synchronized "in time and space" with the radar data: the camera contributes exact ball position pre-impact, marker-less **impact location on the clubface** (impact height/offset), and "4D silhouette clubhead tracking," with clubhead position "measured with the combination of radar and the camera's exact knowledge of the club position in every frame – and in-between frames" (https://www.trackman.com/blog/golf/two-radars-one-camera-zero-doubt; https://support.trackmangolf.com/hc/en-us/articles/36772396790427-TM4-What-is-OERT). TM4's camera: HD 720p@60fps / FHD 1080p@45fps with HDR; indoor use has specified impact-zone lighting requirements (https://www.trackman.com/golf/launch-monitors/tech-specs; https://support.trackmangolf.com/hc/en-us/articles/7001184301851-Trackman-4-Simulator-Impact-Lighting-Requirements). **TrackMan iO** (indoor-only, ceiling/overhead form factor) goes further: 24 GHz radar + dual cameras, one high-speed **up to 4,600 fps** for club and ball tracking plus a patented alignment camera, IR illumination at 810–850 nm, "no minimum space" requirement, with indoor extrapolation grounded in TrackMan's measured-flight database (https://www.trackman.com/golf/launch-monitors/tech-specs). TrackMan holds a patent family on radar+image 3D tracking (e.g., US 10,596,416 / 11,697,046 / 12,128,275, "System and method for three dimensional object tracking using combination of radar and image data") (https://patents.justia.com/inventor/fredrik-tuxen). The corresponding non-TrackMan analog is FlightScope's patented **Fusion Tracking** (X3, Mevo+ with Pro Package): synchronized high-speed image processing locks radar data to observed ball motion, chiefly to firm up indoor/short-flight spin and launch (https://thegolfwire.com/326713-2/; https://flightscope.com/products/flightscope-x3c).

## 6. Independent Accuracy Evidence

- **Leach, Forrester et al. 2017** (*Measurement*; Loughborough): 240 shots (driver/7i/wedge), TrackMan Pro IIIe and Foresight GC2+HMT vs a GOM optical benchmark. Ball parameters showed good agreement (e.g., TrackMan clubhead-speed median difference −0.4 mph); **club parameters materially worse for both**; verdict: fit for coaching/fitting, ball data fit for research, club data use with caution (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0263224117305079; OA: https://repository.lboro.ac.uk/articles/journal_contribution/How_valid_and_accurate_are_measurements_of_golf_impact_parameters_obtained_using_commercially_available_radar_and_stereoscopic_optical_launch_monitors_/9562799).
- **TrackMan 4 indoor reliability study** (J. Sports Sciences, 2024): within/between-session reliability in high-level golfers — generally high reliability for primary launch metrics indoors (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2024.2314864).
- **Mevo+ vs TrackMan 4 indoor agreement study** (2025, *Science Direct / open-access at ORO*): consistency and absolute agreement comparison indoors (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772696725000420; https://oro.open.ac.uk/107777 — both paywalled/403 at fetch time; abstract-level only).
- **MyGolfSpy indoor test** (vs GCQuad reference): MLM2PRO among the tightest for launch angle, spin, carry and ball speed at its price, attributed to its camera+radar hybrid; radar-only R10 and Mevo trail it indoors (https://mygolfspy.com/news-opinion/three-of-the-most-accurate-indoor-launch-monitors-and-one-to-avoid/).
- Physics-of-baseball TrackMan documentation (Alan Nathan) is a useful third-party technical description of the same radar platform in baseball (https://baseball.physics.illinois.edu/trackman.html).

**Key syntheses for the review:** (1) Directness hierarchy: ball speed/launch angles (measured) → spin rate (measured via harmonic sidebands, surface-dependent) → spin axis (trajectory-inverted or camera-assisted) → carry (measured outdoors, modeled indoors) → club speed/path/attack angle (measured, reference-point-sensitive) → face angle/dynamic loft (camera-assisted on TM4/OERT-class; D-plane-inverted or ML-estimated elsewhere). (2) The market's convergent evolution: every vendor has independently concluded pure Doppler is insufficient at short range and added optical sensing (OERT, Fusion Tracking, MLM2PRO cameras, Full Swing "vision") or instrumented balls (RCT/RPT). (3) Independent literature consistently validates ball data and cautions on club data.
